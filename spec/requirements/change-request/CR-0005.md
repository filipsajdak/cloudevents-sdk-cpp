# CR-0005: A binary-body opt-in and rvalue entry points, as additions to `ce::v4`

> **Status, 2026-10-21.** The owner approved the opt-in (stage 1) and ruled it an addition, so v0.6.1 stays a patch release.
> The owner then approved the moving entry points (stage 2) after the measurement of a non-JSON binary-mode receive: `from_message_moving`, a new name, in `ce::v4` only, for HTTP, Kafka and NATS.
> The `approved` status of this request covers both stages.
> The failed-call state, the entry points beyond the three `from_message_moving`, and the treatment of JSON bodies are **proposals the owner has not ruled** and are listed under "Open questions for the owner".

## Rule 4 verdicts

v4 is frozen since v0.6.0, as are v3, v2 and v1: only additions and body-only fixes to published declarations (`docs/SPEC.md` section 3 rule 4, `SWR-BUILD-0006`).
This request settled the question before any code; the analysis is under "Rule 4 analysis".

| # | change | verdict | consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | a new trailing member `parse_binary_body` on the shared `decode_options` | **An addition. Ruled by the owner on 2026-10-21.** The analysis agreed: no published text documents the struct's arity, its decomposition or its size, and `D-JSON-5` documents that it "gains members without breaking a call". The owner accepted the consequences listed under "Owner decisions" and the counter-argument, recorded under "Rule 4 analysis". | v0.6.1 stays a patch release |
| b | `from_message(message&&)` as a same-name overload | **Not safe to treat as an addition (analysis).** `from_message<C>(std::move(m))` compiles today, binds to `const message&` and leaves `m` intact; the overload would move from `m`, the class of change the owner ruled a break on 2026-09-28. It also makes `&from_message<C>` ambiguous. **Declined by the owner on 2026-10-21**, who ruled a new name instead (verdict c). | no change to any published declaration or behaviour |
| c | `from_message_moving` as a new name in each v4 binding, with `read_body_moving` under them | **An addition. Ruled by the owner on 2026-10-21.** A new entity under a new name: no existing call can bind to it, no existing declaration changes, and no address-of or overload-resolution use of an existing name changes. | stage 2 adds names to `ce::v4` only |

## What prompted this

v0.6.0 made a binary-mode body under a JSON media type a parsed `json_document` (`SWR-BIND-0006`, `SWR-BIND-0008`, CR-0004 item 5).
A consumer that only forwards events, such as a router or a gateway, pays that parse and never reads the payload.
v0.6.0 measured the cost on the HTTP full-event path: 15% (RapidJSON) to 54% (nlohmann) more instructions than v3, and 8 to 27 more allocations by codec; the v3 path costs about 36k instructions.
`json::decode_options{.retain_document_up_to = 0}` does not help, because the body is still parsed to validate it (`SWR-BIND-0007`).
The v0.6.0 notes list this as a known limitation, and `ce::v3`, which stores the body unparsed, is the only way out today.

On 2026-10-11 the owner ruled "No, leave it" on an option to skip the parse.
On 2026-10-20 the owner reversed that ruling and approved two additions in principle, in the post-v0.6.0 plan.
On 2026-10-21 the owner answered the open questions: the opt-in is approved and is an addition, and the moving entry points wait for a measurement.
Later on 2026-10-21 the measurement arrived (pull request #93, under item 4) and the owner approved the moving entry points under the name `from_message_moving`, in v4 only.
This request is the first record of the 2026-10-11 ruling in the tree (a grep of `docs/` and `spec/` found no earlier text), and it records the reversal with its date.

CR-0004 item 4 already said that "rvalue entry points such as `from_message(message&&)` may be added later, as additions".
Item 4 ruled on the type of `message::body`, and this request keeps that type.

## What is requested

Items 1 to 3 and 8 are decided: the owner's decision in principle of 2026-10-20, confirmed on 2026-10-21.
Items 4, 6 and 7 are decided by the owner's ruling of 2026-10-21 (the shape, the name, the generations); item 5, the state of the message after a failed call, is a **proposal not yet ruled** (open question 1).

**1. A forwarder opt-in on the shared `decode_options`.**
`decode_options` gains one trailing member, working name `parse_binary_body`, of type `bool`, defaulting to `true`:

```cpp
struct decode_options {
  static constexpr std::size_t default_retention_limit = std::size_t{16} * 1024;
  std::size_t retain_document_up_to = default_retention_limit;
  bool parse_binary_body = true;   // new, last
};
```

With the default, nothing changes: a binary-mode body under a JSON media type is parsed and validated, stored as a `json_document` within the retention limit and as `json_text` above it, and an invalid body fails with `parse_error`.
With `.parse_binary_body = false`, a non-empty binary-mode body under a media type `is_json_content_type` accepts is stored as `json_text` holding the body's bytes exactly as received, **without being parsed or validated**.
It never fails with `parse_error` and costs no parse, whatever its size and whatever `retain_document_up_to` says.
An empty body still carries no payload (`SWR-BIND-0010`), and a body under any other media type is carried as bytes, as before.

The struct stays one shared type, declared once in `ce::v3::json` and brought into `ce::v4::json`.

**2. Which entry points honour it, decided from the code.**
`binding::read_body` is the only reader of a binary-mode body, and the binary path of each `from_message` is its only caller.
Every other entry point that takes `decode_options` reads JSON text and never reaches a binary body, so each accepts the field and ignores it.

| entry point | generation | takes `decode_options` | binary-mode body | the field |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `http::from_message<Codec>`, binary mode | v4 | yes | read by `read_body` | **honoured** |
| `kafka::from_message<Codec>`, binary mode | v4 | yes | read by `read_body` | **honoured** |
| `nats::from_message<Codec>`, binary mode | v4 | yes | read by `read_body` | **honoured** |
| `binding::read_body<Codec>` | v4 | yes | it is the reader | **honoured** |
| `from_message` of each binding, structured mode | v4 | yes | none: the body is the whole event | ignored |
| `http::from_batch_message<Codec>` | v4 | yes | none: the body is a JSON array of events | ignored |
| `nats::from_payload<Codec>` | v4 | yes | none: the payload is the whole event | ignored |
| `json_format::decode`, `decode_batch`, `decode_as`, `decode_batch_as` | v4 and v3 | yes | none: they read an event's JSON text | ignored |
| `json_format::from_value`, `from_value_as` | v4 and v3 | no | none | unchanged: no options to read |
| `from_message`, `from_batch_message`, `from_payload`, `read_body` | v3 | **no** | v3 stores a JSON body as unparsed `json_text` | the field cannot reach them |

A forwarder that receives events in structured mode still pays the full parse; the opt-in is for binary mode, which is the mode a forwarder that does not read the payload should use.
That is stated in the guide and in the doc comment of the field.
The owner ruled on 2026-10-21 that the name `parse_binary_body` stays and that the field applies to binary mode only.

**3. v3 and the field.**
No v3 entry point takes `decode_options` where a binary body is read: `ce::v3::http::from_message(const message&)`, `ce::v3::kafka::from_message(const message&)`, `ce::v3::nats::from_message(const message&)` and `ce::v3::nats::from_payload(std::string_view)` take none, and the v3 `read_body(const binary&, const std::optional<datacontenttype>&)` stores a JSON body as `json_text` without parsing, as v0.5.0 did.
v3 therefore already behaves as `.parse_binary_body = false` would make v4 behave, and nothing in v3 changes.
The only v3 functions that accept the struct are the structured-only `json_format::decode`, `decode_batch`, `decode_as` and `decode_batch_as`, where a binary body does not exist.
The field's doc comment says that v3 ignores it, and `ADR-0013` says the same.
No v3 declaration changes and no v3 behaviour changes.

**4. Rvalue entry points: a new name (approved 2026-10-21).**
A moving entry point takes the message by rvalue reference and moves the body into the stored payload instead of copying it.
The owner ruled on 2026-10-21 that the shape is a new name, `from_message_moving<Codec>(message&&, decode_options = {})`, in the HTTP, Kafka and NATS bindings (`http::from_message_moving<Codec, Values>` keeps the value-policy template parameter), and a matching `binding::read_body_moving<Codec>(binary&&, ...)` in the binding core (the core function is carried over from the analysis; the owner named the three binding entry points, see open question 4).
The three variants returning `result<event>` are otherwise identical to `from_message`: same modes, same errors, same `decode_options`, including `parse_binary_body`.

What a move can and cannot save, from the code (`message::body` is `std::vector<std::byte>`, `json_text::raw` is `std::string`, and CR-0004 item 4 keeps `body` as it is):

| binary-mode body | what `from_message` does | what a move can save |
|---|---|---|
| non-empty, not a JSON media type | copies the bytes into the `ce::binary` alternative | **one allocation and one copy of the body** |
| JSON media type, parsed (default) | parses a view of the body and keeps the DOM or a `std::string` copy above the limit | nothing: the parse reads the bytes, and `std::string` cannot adopt a `std::vector<std::byte>` |
| JSON media type, `.parse_binary_body = false` | copies the bytes into `json_text::raw` | nothing, for the same reason |
| structured mode | parses the body text | nothing |

So the saving is real only for a non-JSON binary-mode body, such as an image, a protobuf or an archive; a forwarder of JSON events gets nothing from it, and the opt-in of item 1 is the part that helps that case.
Headers have no extraction API, so attribute values are copied.
The owner chose on 2026-10-21 to measure before deciding, as for `message::body` in CR-0004.
The measurement is pull request #93 (perf job, gcc-14, x86-64, Callgrind; nlohmann as the codec argument, because `read_body` returns before the codec for a non-JSON media type).
The saving is estimated as the cost of the body copy, an upper bound, since no rvalue entry exists yet:

| body | instructions saved at most | allocations saved | allocated bytes saved |
|---|---|---|---|
| 256 B, `application/octet-stream` | 668 to 672 of 8,647 to 12,109, that is 5.5% to 7.8% | 1 of 4 | 256 of 342 to 354, about 73% to 75% |
| 256 B, `text/plain` (HTTP) | 480 of 10,473, 4.6% | 1 of 3 | 256 of 323, 79.3% |
| 64 KiB, `application/octet-stream` | 66,358 to 66,374 of 74,349 to 77,799, that is 85% to 89% | 1 of 4 | 65,536 of 65,622 to 65,634, 99.9% |
| 64 KiB, `text/plain` (HTTP) | 66,218 of 76,211, 86.9% | 1 of 3 | 65,536 of 65,603, 99.9% |

The instruction figures for 64 KiB are overstated: Callgrind replaces `memcpy` with a routine that costs about one instruction per byte, which an AVX `memcpy` does not.
The allocation and byte figures are exact.
The percentages are a ceiling, as a real move still pays for the call and the checks, and they count the receive only, not what the application does with the body afterwards.
In short: a small body gains little, and a large one saves one allocation and nearly all its bytes.

Entry points that do not get a moving form:

- `http::from_batch_message` parses a JSON array and keeps no bytes of the body, apart from `json_text` slices above the limit that are copied out of it, so a move saves nothing.
- `nats::from_payload` takes a `std::string_view`, which owns nothing to move.
- `json_format` functions take text, not a message.

**5. The state of the message after a moving call (the failed-call state is PROPOSED, not yet ruled by the owner).**
After a successful `from_message_moving`, `header_fields` is unchanged and `body` is valid and unspecified, as a moved-from `std::vector` is: a caller must not read it.
**Proposed, not ruled:** a failed call leaves the whole message unchanged, so a forwarder can still send the bytes to a dead-letter queue.
For the strong guarantee, the body is taken only once nothing can fail; the implementing stage shows how, and if the event's final validation cannot be ordered ahead of the move, the stage reports it and the owner decides between a copy on that path and the weaker guarantee (open question 5).
`from_message(const message&)` never modifies its argument, and a test pins that.

**6. Which generations get the moving entry points (ruled by the owner on 2026-10-21).**
v4 only.
v3 does not get them (declined; an addition to a frozen generation that rule 4 would admit): a v3 consumer that wants fewer copies migrates to v4, and v3's read path copies JSON bodies into `json_text` whatever the entry point.
v2 and v1 do not get them either.

**7. How the two additions interact.**
`from_message_moving` takes `decode_options`, so `.parse_binary_body = false` works with it.
For a JSON media type, the move saves nothing in either setting (the table above).
For a non-JSON media type, `parse_binary_body` has no effect and the move saves the copy.
The two additions therefore cover different bodies: the opt-in helps a forwarder of JSON, the move helps a forwarder of anything else.

**8. The frozen-API diff, and the module.**
The owner ruled on 2026-10-21 that a trailing data member with a default member initializer appended to an aggregate counts as an addition, and the `frozen-api` job (`SWR-BUILD-0015`, merged in #88) is being told the same, so the job passes the new member of `decode_options` without a per-member allowlist entry.
The ruling is recorded in ADR-0013, and `SWR-BUILD-0006` is not amended by this request (follow-up below).
The module (`cloudevents.cppm`) exports `decode_options` by its name, so the new member needs no edit there.
Stage 2 adds a new entry-point name, which needs one `using` per binding in the module and an entry in `test/module_test.cpp`'s exported-name list.
No code changes in this request, so the frozen-API diff (`python3 scripts/frozen_api_diff.py --base v0.6.0`) is unaffected by it; the implementing stage runs it.

## Rule 4 analysis

Evidence was collected on 2026-10-20 at `main` `8fec04f`, before any code.

### a. A new member on `decode_options`

What stops compiling or changes:

| use | result with two members | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| `decode_options{.retain_document_up_to = n}` | compiles | built with GCC 16.2, Apple clang 21 and upstream clang 23.1 at `-std=c++20 -Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic -Wconversion -Wshadow -Werror`: no warning, no error, because the new member has a default member initializer |
| `decode_options{n}` and `decode_options{}` | compile | same three compilers, same flags |
| `auto [limit] = options;` | **stops compiling**: "type decomposes into 2 elements, but only 1 name was provided" (clang), "only 1 name provided for structured binding" (GCC) | same file, built with `-DBIND` |
| `sizeof(decode_options)` | grows from 8 to 16 bytes on LP64 | by inspection of the layout |
| a v3 caller sets `.parse_binary_body = false` | compiles, and has no effect | v3 binary decode takes no options (item 3) |

Greps over `include`, `test`, `examples`, `interop`, `bench`, `fuzz`, `docs` and `README.md`:

- No structured binding of a `decode_options`, and no `std::is_aggregate`, `sizeof` or `tuple_size` use of it (none matched).
- Every construction in the tree is a designated initializer on `retain_document_up_to` (`fuzz/fuzz_json_decode.cpp:20`, `fuzz/v3/fuzz_json_decode.cpp:20`, `test/binding_decode_options.hpp` users, `test/typed_payload_test.cpp`) or `{}`; each stays valid.
- `docs/GUIDE.md` documents the struct only through the designated-initializer spelling, the 16 KiB default and "set per call" (lines 393, 535 and 804). It does not document its arity, its size or decomposition.
- `docs/DECISIONS.md` `D-JSON-5` documents the opposite of a fixed arity: "A plain aggregate is written in one designated initializer at the call site, gains members without breaking a call".
  CR-0001 chose an options aggregate for the same reason.
- `SWR-JSON-0040` and `SWR-BIND-0009` state the `retain_document_up_to` member and the default, and neither says the struct has one member.

Recommendation, then ruling: **an addition.** The owner ruled so on 2026-10-21.
`SWR-BUILD-0006` asks whether a change alters a published declaration or behaviour that published documentation states.
No behaviour any program can observe changes at the default, because `parse_binary_body` defaults to `true`, which is today's behaviour.
No documentation states what the new member would change, and the one text that speaks to growth (`D-JSON-5`) says members may be added without breaking a call.
The decomposition case is a source break for code the documentation never offered, and the tree has none.

Strongest counter-argument:
the struct definition is itself a published declaration, and `SWR-BUILD-0006` says a change that "alters a published declaration" goes to a new namespace; a new data member alters its size and its decomposition, which the earlier rule-4 additions did not (`D-CORE-11` added a friend declaration and `make_string` added a static member function, and neither changes layout).
A user may also read "the `decode_options` of v3 and v4 is one type" to mean that v3 now carries a field it ignores.
The reply is that v3 code which compiled keeps compiling with every documented spelling and behaves the same, but the reply rests on the reading that the documentation, and not the declaration, is the contract; that reading is the owner's to confirm.
The owner accepted the counter-argument as a consequence and ruled that a trailing data member with a default member initializer appended to an aggregate is an addition.
Had the owner read rule 4 literally, the member would have been a break, v0.6.1 would not be a patch, and a v4-only struct would still have been unavailable, because it would replace a published v4 declaration.

### b. `from_message(message&&)` (declined; the new name of verdict c was chosen)

What changes with a same-name overload:

| use | today | with the overload | evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `from_message<C>(std::move(m))`, then read `m` | binds to `const message&`, `m` intact | binds to `message&&`, `m.body` moved-from | built a ten-line model (`/tmp` file, not committed): the program that reads `m.body.size()` afterwards prints 3 without the overload and 0 with it, with no diagnostic at either compiler |
| `auto* f = &from_message<C>;` | compiles | **does not compile**: "incompatible initializer of type '<overloaded function type>'" | same model, `-DADDRESS` |
| `std::function<result<event>(const message&)> f = &from_message<C>;` or any target type | compiles | still compiles: the target type selects the overload | by the overload rules |
| `from_message<C>(request_with(...))` and every other prvalue argument | copies the temporary's body | moves it; nobody can observe the temporary | about 20 call sites in `test/` (`request_with`, `binary_message_with`, `record_with`, `with`) |
| `decltype(from_message<C>(std::declval<const message&>()))` | selects `const message&` | selects `const message&` | `test/http_binding_test.cpp:180`, unchanged |

Greps over `include`, `test`, `examples`, `interop`, `bench`, `fuzz`, `docs` and `README.md`, 340 call sites of `from_message` and `from_batch_message`:

- **No call passes `std::move(...)`** (a grep of `from_message|from_batch_message|from_payload` lines against `std::move|std::forward` matched nothing), and no `std::move` of a variable named like a message appears next to one.
- No address-of or other use of the name without a call (`&...from_message`, matched nothing outside strings and comments).
- No example, bench, interop program or document calls it with an rvalue other than a temporary.
- `docs/GUIDE.md` and `README.md` say nothing about moving a message into a decode (a grep for "moved-from", "std::move(...message" and "message&&" found only the `json_document` and conversion passages).

Does it count as a break?
Two readings give two answers, and the owner decides which holds:

- By the text of `SWR-BUILD-0006` (a published declaration, or behaviour that published documentation states): **no**. No published text says what `from_message` does to an rvalue argument, and no test pins it. This is unlike CR-0004's move, whose copy semantics the v0.5.0 guide and a suite stated.
- By the owner's ruling of 2026-09-28 (a program that compiled still compiles and reads a different value, silently): **yes**. `std::move(m)` is the caller's permission to take the value and not a promise to leave it, but a program that moved into a `const message&` call and read `m` afterwards was correct and would change meaning with no diagnostic. The signature, a `const message&` parameter, is the only documentation, and it promises that the argument is not modified.

The address-of ambiguity is a second, compile-time effect that no reading excuses: a program that spells `auto* f = &from_message<C>` stops compiling.

Alternatives that avoid the hazard:

| alternative | existing calls change | cost | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. same-name overload** `from_message(message&&)` | yes: `std::move` callers silently, address-of callers at compile time | one name; the idiomatic C++ shape | a break by the 2026-09-28 reading |
| **B. a new name**, `from_message_moving` | none | a second name per binding, which the caller must know about; the doc comment of `from_message` points to it | **recommended**: no hazard, greppable, nothing to explain about overload resolution |
| **C. a tag or wrapper**, `from_message<C>(std::move(m), ce::take_body)` or `from_message<C>(ce::owned(std::move(m)))` | none: no existing call can bind to the new parameters | one name, plus a new public tag or wrapper type; the options parameter moves to third place with a tag | workable; costs surface and a type for what a name does |
| **D. take by value**, `from_message(message)` | yes: it replaces a published declaration, and every lvalue call would copy | none | declined: not allowed for a published declaration |

Recommendation: **B**, with C as the alternative if the owner wanted a single name.
**The owner chose B on 2026-10-21, after the measurement, and named it `from_message_moving`.** Declined: the same-name overload (A), the tag or wrapper (C) and the by-value signature (D).

## What this costs

- **A struct that grows.** `decode_options` grows from 8 to 16 bytes and is passed by value to every decoding entry point. It allocates nothing; stage 1 measures the decode and batch operations against main.
- **A source break for code nobody wrote.** `auto [limit] = options;` stops compiling once `decode_options` has two members; the owner accepted it on 2026-10-21.
- **A struct that ce::v3 shares and ignores.** v3 callers can write the field in `json_format::decode` and it does nothing; the doc comment says so.
- **A decode that does not validate.** `.parse_binary_body = false` accepts a body that is not JSON. That is the point of the option, and a caller who opts in takes over that check; the guide states it, and the default stays validating.
- **Stage 2:** a second name per binding with its suite, and one more name in the module's export list. It saves nothing for a JSON body, the case the opt-in serves (the table of item 4), and by the measurement at most 5.5% to 7.8% of the instructions and one of four allocations for a 256 B non-JSON body; the owner approved it on that evidence.

## Alternatives considered and declined

- **A v4-only options struct.**
  It would replace a published v4 declaration, which rule 4 forbids. Declined by the owner on 2026-10-20.
- **A separate `from_message_raw`.**
  It doubles the entry points for a flag, and every option added later would need a second spelling. Declined by the owner on 2026-10-20.
- **Validate without keeping the DOM, as the opt-in.**
  A validation pass is the parse; it saves no instructions, only the retained DOM, which `.retain_document_up_to = 0` already saves. Declined by the owner on 2026-10-20.
- **Keeping the parse as a known limitation.**
  The owner's ruling of 2026-10-11, "No, leave it". Reversed by the owner on 2026-10-20.
- **Skipping the parse in structured mode as well.**
  Structured decode needs the DOM to find `data` and the context attributes, so the event cannot be built without parsing. Not requested.

## Requirements this changes

A changed requirement keeps its current text until the stage that implements the change, so `main` never states behaviour its suites do not verify.
The table says what each statement becomes then.

| requirement | today | after the opt-in (stage 1) | after the moving entry points (stage 2, approved) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `SWR-BIND-0006` | the reader returns a `json_document` built by the codec for a non-empty JSON body | the reader returns a `json_document` for a non-empty JSON body **unless `parse_binary_body` is false, when it returns `json_text`**; the rationale says the parse is the default and not the only behaviour | the rationale names `read_body_moving` |
| `SWR-BIND-0007` | a body above the limit is kept as `json_text`; the rationale says the body "is still parsed above the limit" | unchanged statement; the rationale adds that the parse is skipped only when `parse_binary_body` is false | unchanged |
| `SWR-BIND-0008` | a non-empty body that is not JSON under a JSON media type is a `parse_error` | the statement gains "when `parse_binary_body` is true": **the parse becomes the default, and no longer the only behaviour** | unchanged |
| `SWR-BIND-0009` | every entry point takes `json::decode_options`, defaulting to 16 KiB, and applies it to every JSON payload it decodes | the statement names the second member and its default; an entry point that reads no binary body ignores it | `from_message_moving` is one of the entry points |
| `SWR-JSON-0040` | the retention limit is a decode option | unchanged statement; `decode_options.hpp` gains a member, and `D-JSON-5` records it | unchanged |
| `SWR-HTTP-0004` | `http::from_message(message, codec)` returns a result | unchanged statement | unchanged (the moving sibling is the new `SWR-HTTP-0018`) |
| `SWR-BUILD-0006` | a change to a published declaration or documented behaviour goes to a new namespace | unchanged statement; this request is its fourth use, and the owner's verdicts of this request are recorded in its rationale | unchanged |

| requirement | states | planned evidence |

## Open questions for the owner

Answered on 2026-10-21 and removed: whether the new member is an addition (yes), the name and reach of the opt-in (`parse_binary_body`, binary mode only), whether the frozen-API diff job needs an allowlist entry (no: the rule below), whether `SWR-BUILD-0006` is amended here (no: follow-up), whether to proceed with stage 2 after the measurement (yes), the shape and name of the moving entry point (`from_message_moving`), and whether v3 gets it (no, v4 only).
Still open, none of them ruled; each carries the recommendation the requirement currently states:

1. **The failed-call state (PROPOSED, NOT yet ruled).** The message unchanged after a failed moving call (recommended; `SWR-BIND-0016` states it; the body is taken after the last fallible step, and if the final validation of the event cannot be ordered ahead of the move, the stage reports it and the owner chooses between a copy on that path and the weaker guarantee), or valid and unspecified (simpler, and a forwarder could not send a refused body to a dead-letter queue).
2. **The moved-from state wording.** `SWR-BIND-0015` says that after success the headers are unchanged and the body is "valid and unspecified", as a moved-from `std::vector`. Is that the intended promise, or should the body be specified as empty? Specified-empty binds the library to a move that clears it.
3. **JSON bodies.** The move saves nothing for a body under a JSON media type (the parse reads the bytes and `json_text::raw` is a `std::string`). Proposed: `from_message_moving` still exists for every body and a JSON body takes the copying path, so one name works for all bodies and returns what `from_message` returns. The alternatives are to refuse a JSON body or to leave the case undocumented; the proposal costs nothing at the call site, and `SWR-BIND-0014` is worded that way.
4. **Which other entry points get a moving form.** The owner named HTTP, Kafka and NATS `from_message_moving`. Proposed: `binding::read_body_moving` exists in the core beneath them (as `read_body` does beneath `from_message`); `http::from_batch_message` and `nats::from_payload` get none, because they keep no bytes of the input to move (a `std::string_view` owns nothing). Confirm, or name them.
5. **Structured mode.** A structured-mode message parses its body text, so the move saves nothing and the call takes the copying path. Proposed: the same single-name treatment as question 3. Confirm.

Follow-up, not in this pull request: amend `SWR-BUILD-0006`.
Proposed wording, for a later request to decide: append "A change that only appends a data member with a default member initializer to an aggregate, or only adds an entity under a new name, is an addition and does not require a new version namespace." The first clause is the owner's ruling of 2026-10-21; the second is not ruled and is offered for decision.

## Owner decisions

On 2026-10-20, in principle, in the post-v0.6.0 plan:

1. **Add an opt-in to skip the binary-mode parse.** It reverses the ruling of 2026-10-11, "No, leave it". The default stays "validate and parse".
2. **The opt-in is a new trailing field on `decode_options`**, working name `parse_binary_body`. Declined: a separate `from_message_raw`; validate-only, which saves no instructions.
3. **The field lives on the shared struct, and v3 entry points ignore it.** Declined: a v4-only struct, which would replace a published v4 declaration.
4. **Rvalue entry points are added**, in the shape the open questions settle.
5. **One v0.6.1 carries both**, if rule 4 allows a patch release.

On 2026-10-21, answering the open questions:

6. **The trailing member is an addition**, so v0.6.1 stays a patch release. The owner accepted its consequences: `decode_options` grows from 8 to 16 bytes; `auto [limit] = options;` stops compiling, in code the documentation never offered and the tree does not contain; and v3 ignores the field. The counter-argument found, that the struct definition is itself a published declaration whose size and decomposition a data member changes, is recorded under "Rule 4 analysis".
7. **The rule for the frozen-API diff:** a trailing data member with a default member initializer, appended to an aggregate, counts as an addition. The frozen-API diff job is told the same.
8. **`parse_binary_body` keeps its name and applies to binary mode only:** binary-mode `from_message` in HTTP, Kafka and NATS, and `read_body`. Other entry points accept the field and ignore it.
9. **The moving entry points: measure first.** Stage 2 is conditional on a measurement of a non-JSON binary-mode receive and a further owner decision. Superseded by items 11 to 13 below.
10. **`SWR-BUILD-0006` is not amended here.** The wording proposed above is a follow-up.

Later on 2026-10-21, after the measurement of pull request #93 (figures under item 4):

11. **Stage 2 goes ahead.** The moving entry points are added.
12. **The shape and name: `from_message_moving`, a new name**, in the HTTP, Kafka and NATS bindings. Declined: a same-name `from_message(message&&)` overload, a tag or wrapper parameter, and a by-value `from_message(message)`. Existing calls do not change, so it is an addition.
13. **v4 only.** v3 does not get the moving entry points (declined).

Not ruled, listed under "Open questions for the owner": the state of the message after a failed call (the recommendation, "unchanged", is a proposal), the moved-from wording, the treatment of JSON and structured bodies, and which entry points besides the three.
